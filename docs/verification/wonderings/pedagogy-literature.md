## 1. The central question: plausible but unstudied

**No study has ever supplied inquiry questions to learners and measured their own
question-generation as an outcome.** Systematic ERIC, OpenAlex, Crossref and
Semantic Scholar queries returned zero; nothing in WISE, Inq-ITS, Go-Lab,
SCY-Lab, BGuILE, Betty's Brain, iSTART or AutoTutor.

So the suppression risk is **untested**, not established — and not refuted. The
plan's "recorded, not mitigated" reads as established-and-unaddressed, which
overstates it.

**Chin & Osborne (2008) does not address it.** 250 citation contexts contain zero
suppression or crowding-out language; the concern there is that student questions
are *rare*, not displaced. For rarity cite **Graesser & Person (1994)** instead:
classroom student questions run **0.11–0.17 per student per hour**, and **96% of
classroom questions are the teacher's**. Their finding that question *quality*
correlated with achievement while *frequency* did not is directly relevant to
what this panel should measure.

**The assistance dilemma licenses no prediction either way.** Koedinger, Pavlik,
McLaren & Aleven (2008) concede the inverted-U rests on a single study and that
the three-condition bracketing study "has not been performed." The replicated
finding is **equal learning more efficiently**, not worse learning. Do not cite
VanLehn (2011) for "assistance hurts" — the interaction plateau is a plateau.

## 2. The closest analogues point *away* from suppression

- **Yu, Tsai & Wu (2013)**, *AJET* 29(3), 416–433 — the most on-point study found.
  N = 78 fifth-graders, 8 sessions. Scaffold was **14 generic question stems each
  paired with a context-specific worked example, with an IMPORT button** —
  architecturally very close to this panel. Question quality **including
  originality** rose: totals 46.11 (none) / **74.19 (immediate)** / 52.32
  (delayed), F = 5.99, p = .004, η² = .138.
  Also: group differences washed out from session 5; on withdrawal the
  longer-exposed group kept rising while the shorter fell (suggestive only,
  p = .178); and **withholding the scaffold to force struggle failed outright** —
  the delayed group never differed from no-scaffold in any of eight sessions.
- **Rosenshine, Meister & Chapman (1996)**, *RER* 66(2), 181–221 — **generic
  question stems were the most effective prompt type in the review, ES = 1.12.**
  Four sharp qualifications: every effect size is a *comprehension* score, none is
  question quality or quantity; the stem cell rests on 4 studies, 3 by one author;
  **not one of those four assessed independent question-asking against a
  comparison group**; and the best head-to-head test (King & Rosenshine 1993,
  ES 1.25) was excluded because students worked with the stems still in hand.
- **Tetzlaff et al. (2025)**, *Learning and Instruction* 98 — 60 randomized
  experiments, N = 5,924. Low-prior-knowledge secondary learners do better with
  **more** assistance, d ≈ +0.41: *"rather provide assistance than withhold it
  when in doubt."* Expertise reversal is domain-relative, not age-relative.
- **Kuang, Eysink & de Jong (2020)**, *JCAL* 36(4) — N = 52, mean age 13.9.
  Supplying half a hypothesis improved post-test and **did not degrade the half
  students wrote themselves** (p = .41).

## 3. The real risk is diversity and ownership, not quantity or quality

Three independent literatures supply a mechanism:

- **Pedagogical sampling — Bonawitz et al. (2011)**, *Cognition* 120(3). Children
  shown one function discovered fewer others (0.72 vs 1.15–1.30, F(1,81) = 4.58);
  fewer unique actions (4.00 vs 5.30–6.15, F(1,81) = 9.39). *"A teacher's failure
  to provide evidence for additional functions provides evidence for their
  absence."* **The narrowing appeared only in the pedagogical condition** — naive
  and interrupted demonstrations produced none.
- **Design fixation** (Jansson & Smith 1991; Smith, Ward & Schumacher 1993; Kohn &
  Smith 2011) — examples narrow *what* is generated but, in most studies,
  **do not reduce volume**.
- **LLM homogenization** — Doshi & Hauser (2024) *Science Advances* 10(28):
  AI-assisted work rated more creative but *"more similar to each other."*
  Anderson, Shah & Kreminski (2024): users produced **less semantically distinct**
  ideas, **more** of them, and *"felt less responsible for the ideas they
  generated."*

**For a tool whose goal is exploration across a whole dataset, narrowing which
columns get looked at is the failure mode that matters — not silence.**

## 4. Two findings that dissolve the framing

- **Leahy et al. (2020)**, *J. Mechanical Design* 142(10), N = 185 — contrary to
  their own hypothesis, students fixated **more on their own first idea (0.32)
  than on a provided example (0.24)**. **There is no example-free baseline.** A
  student with no panel is not exploring an unconstrained space; they anchor on
  their own first thought, harder.
- **George & Wiley (2020)**, *Memory & Cognition* 48(2) — **framing determines the
  sign.** Examples plus "go past these" *enhanced* originality; bare exposure did
  nothing; vivid exposure caused fixation. Converging: **Gweon et al. (2014)** —
  children explore **more broadly** when they know a source omitted something.

> *"Here are three of many — what else is in here?"* is a measurably different
> stimulus from *"here are the questions worth asking."* This is where §9.3's
> deliberate fallibility earns its keep — as **framing**, not as an error rate.

## 5. Counter-evidence: modeling does teach question-asking

**Palincsar & Brown (1984)** — main-idea questions 54% → 70%, unclear questions
20% → 4%; students' questions became *"inventions rather than selections."*
Transfer to independent generation t(20) = 4.58, p < .001, which the authors call
*"modest but reliable."* **The overlooked negative result:** a control that helped
students *answer* provided questions produced *"no reliable improvement."*
**Consuming supplied questions was inert where producing them was transformative.**

**Collins, Brown & Newman (1989)** supports modeling and also contains the
sharpest criticism of a permanent panel: *"Exploration is the natural culmination
of the fading of supports. It involves not only fading in problem solving, but
**fading in problem setting as well.**"*

Do not cite Bandura for observational learning of question-asking — ERIC returns
zero.

## 6. The tension the research did not resolve: fading

- **For fading:** McNeill et al. (2006) — but p = .07, d ≈ 0.24–0.27. Yu et al.'s
  withdrawal divergence — but p = .178. Collins/Brown/Newman is theory.
- **Against:** Belland et al. (2017), 333 outcomes — fading vs not fading
  **p > .05**, *"fading as defined in the scaffolding literature is not
  necessary."* Wecker & Fischer (2011), Martin et al. (2019) show fading
  **actively harming** when nothing absorbs the withdrawal.

**What survives both:** make support **adaptive to demonstrated competence, not
scheduled**, and **give the learner a dismiss control** — self-selected withdrawal
scored **g = 0.519**, the highest-performing customization method in Kim et al.
(2018), ahead of performance-adapted and fixed-schedule. The plan's global toggle
is closer to right than a timer, but **per-wondering dismissal is what the
evidence points at, and it is cheap.**

**And a failure mode nobody measured:** Nückles et al. (2010) found **both
permanent and faded prompting produced substantial motivation decreases over
time.** Fading did not rescue motivation. For an ambient companion that is the
risk worth watching.

## 8. §13.2 is answerable after all

The plan records question-suppression as unmeasurable by uptake. It is measurable
— just not by uptake. Pea's **Clever Hans challenge** frames it as a measurement
obligation: with the panel on, you cannot tell whether the student or the panel is
doing the noticing. The design is a **periodic panel-off condition** plus
**question/exploration diversity measured across students** — the outcome none of
the education studies collected.

That converges exactly with review decision **D1**: three document reviewers
independently found the uptake metric measures compliance; this says the outcome
worth measuring is diversity and ownership. Both point at the same replacement.
